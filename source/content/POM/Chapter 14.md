# Chapter 14: Managing Communication
## Robbins & Coulter - Management

## 1. Understanding Communication (LO 14.1)

### Definition
- **Communication** = the transfer and understanding of meaning
- Transfer alone is not enough -- meaning must be understood by the receiver
- Good communication is NOT defined as agreement; disagreement can coexist with clear understanding
- Two types:
  - **Interpersonal communication**: between two or more people
  - **Organizational communication**: all patterns, networks, and systems within an organization

### Functions of Communication (5 major functions)
1. **Management/Control** - Manages member behavior through authority hierarchies, formal guidelines, and informal group norms
2. **Motivation** - Clarifies what employees should do, how well they are doing, and how they can improve; tied to goal-setting and feedback
3. **Emotional Sharing** - Primary social interaction mechanism; lets members express frustration, satisfaction, feelings
4. **Persuasion** - Can be positive (commitment to values) or negative (breaking rules for organizational goals)
5. **Information Exchange** - Provides information individuals and groups need to make decisions

---

## 2. The Communication Process (LO 14.2)

### Seven Elements
1. **Source** - the communication origin / sender
2. **Message** - the purpose to be conveyed
3. **Encoding** - converting a message into symbolic form
4. **Channel** - the medium the message travels along
5. **Decoding** - the receiver retranslates the sender's message
6. **Receiver** - the recipient of the message
7. **Feedback** - the receiver's response back to the sender

### Noise
- Any disturbance that interferes with message transmission
- Can occur at any point in the process
- Examples: illegible print, telephone static, inattention, background machinery sounds, low speaking voice (internal), loud coworkers (external)

---

## 3. Modes of Communicating

### Oral Communication
- Speeches, formal one-on-one/group discussions, informal grapevine
- **Advantages**: speed, rapid feedback, social/cultural/emotional exchange; builds trust and cooperation across cultural boundaries
- **Disadvantage**: distortion increases with number of people in the chain; oral "chains" are a liability in large organizations

### Written Communication
- Letters, email, instant messaging, blogs, periodicals, PowerPoint, text messaging, social media, apps
- Creates a record; generally more deliberate and precise than oral

### Nonverbal Communication
- Body movements, intonations/emphasis, facial expressions
- Every body movement has meaning; no movement is entirely accidental (though some are unconscious)
- People read more about attitude and emotion from nonverbal cues than from words
- When nonverbal cues conflict with verbal messages, listeners often believe the nonverbal cues
- Absence of nonverbal record (e.g., reading meeting minutes) loses significant meaning
- Facial expressions convey: arrogance, aggressiveness, fear, shyness, and other characteristics

## 4. Barriers to Effective Communication

### 1. Information Overload
- Occurs when information exceeds our processing capacity
- Average worker spends ~13 hours/week dealing with emails
- Responses to overload: ignore, forget, selectively process, or stop communicating entirely

### 2. Filtering
- Deliberate manipulation of information to make it appear more favorable to the receiver
- Telling the manager what they want to hear = filtering
- Condensing information as it moves up organizational levels = filtering
- More vertical levels = more filtering opportunities
- Collaborative structures reduce filtering; email reduces filtering (more direct)
- Organizational cultures that reward style/appearance over substance encourage filtering

### 3. Emotions
- Extreme emotions hinder rational, objective thinking
- People who feel threatened become defensive: verbal attacks, sarcasm, excessive judgment, questioning motives
- Emotional state of receiver affects how they interpret a message

### 4. Language
- Same word can mean different things to different people
- Age, education, cultural background all influence language and word definitions
- **Jargon** = specialized terminology used within a group; helps within the group, creates barriers outside it
- Even employees in the same organization but different departments use different jargon

### 5. Silence
- Absence of communication is easy to overlook but is itself a message
- Can signal noninterest, unwillingness to address a topic, feeling of powerlessness, mistreatment, or negative emotions
- Can also result from information overload or deliberation
- Using silence and withholding communication are common and problematic

### 6. National Culture
- Every aspect of global communication is influenced by cultural differences
- Japanese: prefer face-to-face over written despite technology access; extensive consultation before formal documentation
- US managers: rely heavily on formal written communication (reports, memos)
- Latin American, Arab, Asian executives: leave messages unspecified; rely on context and nonverbal cues
- German managers: expect messages to be explicit and specific
- Cultural differences act as distortions to effective communication

## 5. Overcoming Communication Barriers (5 strategies)

### 1. Use Feedback
- Ask questions to verify the message was received as intended
- Ask the receiver to restate the message in their own words
- Feedback can be nonverbal (e.g., non-compliance with an instruction = implicit feedback)
- Look for nonverbal cues

### 2. Simplify Language
- Tailor language to the specific audience
- Effective communication = message both received AND understood
- Jargon is fine within a group that understands it; avoid it outside that group

### 3. Listen Actively
- **Active listening** = listening for full meaning without making premature judgments or interpretations
- Hearing is passive; listening is an active search for meaning
- Average speaking rate: 125-200 words/minute
- Average listening comprehension capacity: up to 400 words/minute
- The gap between the two creates idle brain time and opportunities for distraction
- Active listening demands total concentration

#### Key active listening behaviors:
1. Make eye contact -- focuses attention, reduces distraction, encourages speaker
2. Exhibit affirmative nods and appropriate facial expressions
3. Pay attention to nonverbal cues -- detect mismatch between words and body language
4. Ask questions and paraphrase -- clarifies understanding, assures speaker you are listening; use "What I hear you saying is..." or "Do you mean...?"
5. Make smooth transitions between speaker and listener roles -- don't plan your next statement while the other person is still speaking

### 4. Constrain Emotions
- Emotions cloud and distort communication
- An upset manager is more likely to misconstrue incoming messages and fail to communicate outgoing messages clearly
- Solution: calm down and get emotions under control before communicating

### 5. Watch Nonverbal Cues
- Ensure your actions align with and reinforce your words
- Effective communicators consciously monitor their own nonverbal signals

## 6. Effective Organizational Communication (LO 14.3)

### Formal vs. Informal Communication

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Formal** | Communication within prescribed organizational work arrangements (e.g., manager assigning a task, employee reporting a problem) |
| **Informal** | Communication not defined by the structural hierarchy (e.g., hallway conversations, lunch room talk) |

**Purposes of informal communication:**
1. Satisfies employees' need for social interaction
2. Creates alternative, faster, and more efficient channels of communication that can improve organizational performance

### Direction of Communication Flow

#### Downward Communication
- Flows from manager to employees
- Used to: inform, direct, coordinate, and evaluate
- Examples: assigning goals, job descriptions, policy information, performance evaluation, pointing out problems

#### Upward Communication
- Flows from employees to managers
- Keeps managers aware of employee feelings, job satisfaction, coworker relationships, and organizational health
- Examples: performance reports, suggestion boxes, attitude surveys, grievance procedures, manager-employee discussions, informal group sessions

#### Lateral Communication
- Among employees on the same organizational level
- Important in dynamic environments; saves time and facilitates coordination
- Common in cross-functional teams
- Risk: conflicts arise if managers are not kept informed of lateral decisions and actions

#### Diagonal Communication
- Crosses both work areas and organizational levels simultaneously
- Example: credit analyst communicating directly with regional marketing manager
- Benefits: efficiency, speed
- Facilitated by email (any employee can reach any other, including upper management)
- Downside: often leaves managers out of the loop; can create conflicts

## 7. Communication Networks

Three common patterns combining vertical and horizontal flows:

### 1. Chain Network
- Follows the formal chain of command, both downward and upward
- Speed: Moderate | Accuracy: High | Leader emergence: Moderate | Member satisfaction: Moderate

### 2. Wheel Network
- All communication flows through a central, identifiable leader (the hub)
- Speed: Fast | Accuracy: High | Leader emergence: High | Member satisfaction: Low

### 3. All-Channel Network
- Communication flows freely among all members of the work team
- Speed: Fast | Accuracy: Moderate | Leader emergence: None | Member satisfaction: High

**Key insight**: No single network is best for all situations. Choice depends on goal.

### The Grapevine
- The informal organizational communication network
- Active in almost every organization
- 63% of employees hear about important matters first through the grapevine
- Only ~10% of people act as active liaisons (pass information to more than one person)
- Which individuals are active depends on the message and their level of interest
- Can be used strategically by managers: "planting" messages with key liaisons
- Value: acts as a filter and feedback mechanism; reflects real employee concerns and anxieties
- Today's grapevine vehicle is increasingly social media (Twitter, Facebook, etc.)
- Legal note: employees in private-sector organizations have the right to discuss work conditions freely and without fear of retribution

## 8. Communication in the Internet and Social Media Age (LO 14.4)

### The 24/7 Work Environment
- IT has made continuous connectivity possible regardless of location or time
- Benefits: better monitoring of individual/team performance, faster decisions with more complete information, more collaboration opportunities, full accessibility
- Employees no longer need to be at their desks to communicate

### Social Media in Organizations
- Used for information sharing, team coordination, product demos, announcements
- Internal portals (like private YouTube channels) allow employees to share and engage with company content
- Benefits: reduces email overload by compartmentalizing conversations; enables two-way information sharing and employee suggestions
- **Downside**: highly addictive; ~90% of employees check social media at work; ~1 in 5 check it 10+ times per workday; most checks are for personal use

### Limitations of Digital Communication
- Lacks personal interaction; understanding is harder to achieve in virtual environments
- Generation Z particularly prefers texting/email/social media over face-to-face
- Digital channels are NOT suitable for: emotionally charged messages, sensitive information, confidential matters, complex topics requiring explicit feedback
- Face-to-face communication is still preferred for: complex messages, sensitive topics, building trust, situations requiring confirmed understanding

### Cybersecurity
- Computer networks are not impenetrable
- Data breaches can go undetected for months, creating massive potential damages
- Almost all organizational transactions are now digital: employee info, customer data, financial records, intellectual property
- IoT, smartphones, and cloud computing expand the attack surface
- Risk cannot be fully eliminated but must be managed

#### Essential cybersecurity practices:
- Use secure passwords
- Be suspicious of external downloads and emails
- Back up data regularly
- Secure Wi-Fi networks
- Install and renew complete antivirus, antispyware, and firewall packages on every computer

## 9. Becoming a Better Communicator (LO 14.5)

**Key insight from industry**: LinkedIn survey of 4,000 professionals found soft skills are the #1 priority for talent development; executives rank communication and leadership as the two most important skills employees should learn.

### A. Persuasion Skills
- **Persuasion skills** = skills that enable a person to influence others to change their minds or behavior
- 4 basic elements of effective persuasion:
  1. Be clear about the "who," "what," and "why"
  2. Keep it simple
  3. Think about the other person's needs, motivation, and interests when shaping your argument
  4. Appeal to both "head" (rational) and "heart" (emotional) arguments

### B. Speaking Skills
- **Speaking skills** = ability to communicate information and ideas by talking so others will understand
- 70% of employees who make presentations say good presentation skills are important to career success
- ~20% of survey respondents would avoid making presentations even if it meant "losing respect"
- Characteristics of effective speakers: authenticity, humility, brevity, clear understanding of the audience
- "No one ever complained about a speech being too short"
- Resources: Toastmasters clubs for practicing formal and impromptu speaking

### C. Writing Skills
- **Writing skills** = communicating effectively in text appropriate for its audience
- Rules for clear professional writing:
  - Think before you write -- focus on what you want to say BEFORE starting
  - Be direct -- get to the point quickly
  - Cut the fat -- eliminate unnecessary words
  - Avoid jargon and grandiose language -- strive for clarity, not impression
  - Be professional -- business writing differs from texts, emails, and social media posts; avoid abbreviations, acronyms, and textspeak (especially relevant for Gen Z and millennials)

### D. Reading Skills
- **Reading skills** = understanding written sentences and paragraphs in work-related documents
- Average employee receives ~122 work emails daily, plus memos, reports, policy statements
- If reading comprehension or speed is lacking, consider a reading-improvement course
