## 1. TECHNIQUES FOR ASSESSING THE ENVIRONMENT

### 1.1 Environmental Scanning

**Definition:** Screening large amounts of information to detect emerging trends and anticipate/interpret changes in the environment.

- Establishes the basis for forecasts
- Research shows companies that use environmental scanning have higher performance
- Two fast-growing areas: **competitor intelligence** and **global scanning**

#### Competitor Intelligence

- A process by which organizations gather information about their competitors
- Key questions: Who are they? What are they doing? How will what they're doing affect us?
- Experts suggest 80% of what managers need to know can be found from their own employees, suppliers, customers, and online searches
- Does NOT have to involve spying
- Readily accessible sources: advertisements, promotional materials, press releases, government filings, annual reports, job listings, newspaper reports, industry studies, trade shows, sales force debriefs, reverse engineering of competitor products, competitor websites
- Can also suggest **disengagement** - sometimes intelligence reveals no business case to pursue a competitor

**Legal/Ethical boundary:**
- Competitor intelligence becomes **illegal** when it involves theft of proprietary materials or trade secrets
- The **Economic Espionage Act** makes it a crime in the US to engage in economic espionage or steal a trade secret
- Espionage is a global problem
- Fine line exists between *legal and ethical* vs *legal but unethical*

#### Global Scanning

- Particularly important because world markets are complex and dynamic
- Value depends on the extent of the organization's global activities
- Managers must globalize their perspectives - domestic sources are too limited
- Tools: information-clipping services (review world newspapers and business periodicals), electronic services providing topic searches and automatic updates

### 1.2 Forecasting

**Definition:** Forecasting produces **forecasts** - predictions of outcomes. It is an important part of planning.

Environmental scanning establishes the basis for forecasts.

#### Forecasting Techniques

Two categories: **Quantitative** and **Qualitative**

##### Quantitative Forecasting
Applies a set of mathematical rules to a series of past data to predict outcomes. Preferred when managers have sufficient hard data.

| Technique | Description | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Time series analysis | Fits a trend line to a mathematical equation and projects into the future | Predicting next quarter's sales based on 4 years of previous sales data |
| Regression models | Predicts one variable based on known or assumed other variables | Seeking factors that predict a certain sales level (e.g., price, advertising expenditures) |
| Econometric models | Uses a set of regression equations to simulate segments of the economy | Predicting change in car sales as a result of changes in tax laws |
| Economic indicators | Uses one or more economic indicators to predict a future state of the economy | Using change in GNP to predict discretionary income |
| Substitution effect | Uses a mathematical formula to predict how, when, and under what circumstances a new product or technology will replace an existing one | Predicting the effect of streaming video services on Blu-ray player sales |

##### Qualitative Forecasting
Uses judgment and opinions of knowledgeable individuals to predict outcomes. Used when precise data are limited or hard to obtain.

| Technique | Description | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Jury of opinion | Combines and averages the opinions of experts | Polling HR managers to predict next year's college recruitment needs |
| Sales force composition | Combines estimates from field sales personnel of customers' expected purchases | Predicting next year's sales of industrial lasers |
| Customer evaluation | Combines estimates from established customers' purchases | Surveying major car dealers by a car manufacturer to determine types and quantities desired |

#### CPFR - Collaborative Planning, Forecasting, and Replenishment

- Framework for the flow of information, goods, and services between retailers and manufacturers
- Each organization uses its own data to calculate a demand forecast for a particular product
- If forecasts differ by a certain amount (e.g., 10%), the retailer and manufacturer exchange data and written comments until arriving at a more accurate forecast
- Helps both organizations do a better job of planning

#### Forecasting Effectiveness

- Forecasting accuracy in surveys ranged between 56 and 75 (inventory managers)
- 40% of respondents reported improvement after adopting inventory management software
- 39% of financial executives could only reliably forecast revenues one quarter out
- 16% of financial executives said they were "in the dark" about revenue forecasts
- A company's forecasting ability can be a **distinctive competence**

**Suggestions for making forecasting more effective:**

1. Forecasting techniques are most accurate when the environment is **not rapidly changing** - the more dynamic the environment, the more likely managers will forecast ineffectively
2. Forecasting is relatively **ineffective** in predicting nonseasonal events (recessions, unusual occurrences, discontinued operations, competitor actions/reactions)
3. Use **simple forecasting methods** - they tend to do as well as, and often better than, complex methods that may confuse random data for meaningful information
4. **Involve more people** - at Fortune 100 companies, 1,000 to 5,000 managers provide forecasting input; more people involved = more reliable outcomes
5. **Compare every forecast with "no change"** - a no-change forecast is accurate approximately half the time
6. Use **rolling forecasts** that look 12 to 18 months ahead instead of a single static forecast - helps spot trends better and helps organizations be more adaptive
7. **Do not rely on a single forecasting method** - make forecasts with several models and average them, especially for longer-range forecasts
8. **Do not assume** you can accurately identify turning points in a trend - what appears to be a significant turning point often turns out to be a random event
9. Remember that forecasting is a **managerial skill** that can be practiced and improved - interpreting the forecast and incorporating it into planning decisions is the real challenge

## 2. TECHNIQUES FOR ALLOCATING RESOURCES

**Resources:** An organization's assets - including financial, physical, human, and intangible - that are used to develop, manufacture, and deliver products to customers.

Four techniques covered:
1. Budgeting
2. Scheduling
3. Breakeven Analysis
4. Linear Programming

---

### 2.1 Budgeting

**Budget:** A numerical plan for allocating resources to specific activities.

**Budgeting:** The process of allocating resources to pay for designated future costs.

Managers typically prepare budgets for:
- Revenues
- Expenses
- Large capital expenditures (e.g., equipment)
- Time, space, and use of material resources (using non-dollar numbers: person-hours, capacity utilization, units of production)

**Why are budgets popular?**
- Applicable to a wide variety of organizations and work activities
- Monetary units provide a common measuring unit
- Used regardless of organizational level
- Forces financial discipline and structure throughout the organization

Many managers dislike budgets because they feel the process is time-consuming, inflexible, inefficient, and/or ineffective.

#### Types of Budgets

- **Revenue Budget:** Projects future sales
- **Expense Budget:** Lists primary activities and allocates a dollar amount to each
- **Variable Budget:** Takes into account costs that vary with volume
- **Fixed Budget:** Assumes a fixed level of sales or production
- **Profit Budget:** Combines revenue and expense budgets of various units to determine each unit's profit contribution
- **Cash Budget:** Forecasts cash on hand and how much will be needed

#### How to Improve Budgeting

- Collaborate and communicate
- Be flexible
- Goals should drive budgets - budgets should not determine goals
- Coordinate budgeting throughout the organization
- Use budgeting/planning software when appropriate
- Remember that budgets are tools
- Remember that profits result from smart management, not because you budgeted for them

#### Two Approaches to Setting Budgets

**1. Incremental Budgeting:**
- Starts with the current budget from which managers decide whether additional resources are needed and the justification for requesting them
- Appropriate for adjusting budgets that contain all the necessary expenditures
- Possible drawback: **wasteful spending** - managers learn that if they don't spend their allotted money, they will receive less in the future (e.g., spending budget at year-end just to avoid cuts)

**2. Zero-Base Budgeting (ZBB):**
- Starts with an established point of **zero** rather than using the current budget as the basis
- Managers start with a clean sheet of paper each year and must justify every budget item from scratch
- Helps minimize wasteful spending
- Popularity has increased as companies seek to cut costs and increase competitiveness
- When set in a culture of cost management, ZBB helps managers more carefully scrutinize the value of different practices
- Creates a platform for considering the value of alternative expenditures and helps redirect resources to areas that raise productivity and profits

### 2.2 Scheduling

**Scheduling:** Detailing what activities have to be done, the order in which they are to be completed, who is to do each, and when they are to be completed.

Three scheduling devices: Gantt charts, load charts, PERT network analysis.

#### Gantt Charts

**Definition:** A scheduling chart developed by Henry Gantt (early 1900s) that shows actual and planned output over a period of time.

- Essentially a bar graph
- **Horizontal axis:** Time
- **Vertical axis:** Activities to be scheduled
- Bars show output - both **planned** and **actual** - over a period of time
- Lets managers see what has yet to be done and assess whether an activity is ahead of, behind, or on schedule
- Serves as both a **planning tool** and a **control tool**

#### Load Charts

**Definition:** A modified Gantt chart that schedules capacity by entire departments or specific resources.

- Instead of listing activities on the vertical axis, load charts list entire departments or specific resources
- Allows managers to plan and control **capacity utilization**
- Schedules capacity by work areas
- Helps managers see who is free to take on new work

#### PERT Network Analysis

**Definition (PERT network):** A flowchart diagram showing the sequence of activities needed to complete a project and the time or cost associated with each activity.

- PERT = Program Evaluation and Review Technique
- Appropriate for large, complex projects requiring coordinated inputs from multiple areas
- Requires coordinating activities, some of which must be done simultaneously, some of which can't begin until preceding activities are completed
- Makes it easy to compare the effects alternative actions might have on scheduling and costs
- Allows managers to monitor a project's progress, identify possible bottlenecks, and shift resources as necessary to keep the project on schedule

**Four key terms:**

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Events** | End points that represent the completion of major activities |
| **Activities** | The time or resources needed to progress from one event to another |
| **Slack time** | The amount of time an individual activity can be delayed without delaying the whole project |
| **Critical path** | The longest sequence of activities in a PERT network; any delay here delays the entire project; activities on the critical path have **zero** slack time |

**Steps in Developing a PERT Network:**

1. Identify every significant activity that must be achieved for the project to be completed
2. Determine the order in which these events must be completed
3. Diagram the flow of activities from start to finish - use **circles** for events, **arrows** for activities - this produces the PERT network
4. Compute a time estimate for completing each activity using a weighted average formula (see below)
5. Using the network diagram with time estimates, determine a schedule for start and finish dates for each activity and for the entire project

**Expected Time Formula:**

```
te = (to + 4*tm + tp) / 6
```

Where:
- `te` = expected time
- `to` = optimistic time estimate (ideal conditions)
- `tm` = most likely estimate (normal conditions)
- `tp` = pessimistic estimate (worst possible conditions)

**Worked Example - PERT Expected Time:**

Suppose an activity has:
- Optimistic time (to) = 2 weeks
- Most likely time (tm) = 4 weeks
- Pessimistic time (tp) = 12 weeks

```
te = (2 + 4*4 + 12) / 6
te = (2 + 16 + 12) / 6
te = 30 / 6
te = 5 weeks
```

**Worked Example - Finding the Critical Path:**

Given events A through K with the following paths:
- A-B-C-D-I-J-K = 44 weeks
- A-B-C-D-G-H-J-K = 50 weeks
- A-B-C-E-G-H-J-K = 47 weeks
- A-B-C-F-G-H-J-K = 47 weeks

**Critical path = A-B-C-D-G-H-J-K (50 weeks)** - the longest path.

Any delay in events on A-B-C-D-G-H-J-K delays the entire project.
Events NOT on the critical path (e.g., Event I) have slack time and can be delayed without affecting the final completion date.

To speed up the project, a manager would focus resources on activities along the critical path. Resources can be transferred from non-critical path activities that have slack time.

### 2.3 Breakeven Analysis

**Definition:** A technique for identifying the point at which total revenue is just sufficient to cover total costs.

- Points out the relationship between revenues, costs, and profits
- Can be computed graphically or using a formula

**Key terms:**
- **P** = unit price of the product being sold
- **VC** = variable cost per unit
- **TFC** = total fixed costs
- **Fixed costs:** Expenses that do not change regardless of volume (e.g., insurance premiums, rent, property taxes)
- **Variable costs:** Change in proportion to output (e.g., raw materials, labor costs, energy costs)

**Breakeven Point Formula:**

```
BE = TFC / (P - VC)
```

**Worked Example:**

A photocopying service charges $0.10 per copy. Fixed costs are $27,000/year. Variable costs are $0.04 per copy.

```
BE = 27,000 / (0.10 - 0.04)
BE = 27,000 / 0.06
BE = 450,000 copies
```

Annual revenue at breakeven = 450,000 * $0.10 = **$45,000**

**Uses as a planning tool:**
- Set sales goals based on a desired profit level
- Determine what sales level is needed to reach a profit goal
- Determine how much volume has to increase to break even if currently operating at a loss
- Determine how much volume can be lost and still break even

**Adding a profit target:**

To find the sales volume needed to achieve a specific profit (profit target = PT):

```
Required units = (TFC + PT) / (P - VC)
```

Example: Using the same photocopying business, to achieve a profit of $9,000:
```
Required units = (27,000 + 9,000) / (0.10 - 0.04)
Required units = 36,000 / 0.06
Required units = 600,000 copies
```

### 2.4 Linear Programming

**Definition:** A mathematical technique that solves resource allocation problems.

**Requirements for linear programming to apply:**
1. Resources must be **limited**
2. The goal must be **outcome optimization**
3. Resources can be combined in **alternative ways** to produce a number of output mixes
4. A **linear relationship** must exist between variables - a change in one variable must be accompanied by an exactly proportional change in the other

**Applications:**
- Selecting transportation routes that minimize shipping costs
- Allocating a limited advertising budget among product brands
- Making the optimal assignment of people among projects
- Determining how much of each product to make with limited resources

**Worked Example:**

A manufacturing plant produces two products:
- Potpourri bags: $10 profit per unit, 2 hrs manufacturing, 2 hrs assembly
- Scented candles: $18 profit per unit, 4 hrs manufacturing, 2 hrs assembly
- Monthly capacity: 1,200 hrs manufacturing, 900 hrs assembly

**Step 1 - Define the objective function:**
```
Maximize profit = 10P + 18S
```
Where P = number of potpourri bags, S = number of scented candles.

**Step 2 - Define the constraints:**
```
Manufacturing:  2P + 4S <= 1,200
Assembly:       2P + 2S <= 900
Non-negativity: P >= 0, S >= 0
```

**Step 3 - Find corner points of the feasibility region:**

The optimal solution always lies at a corner (vertex) of the feasibility region. Evaluate profit at each corner:

- Point A (P=0, S=0):   Profit = 10(0) + 18(0) = $0
- Point B (P=0, S=300): Profit = 10(0) + 18(300) = $5,400
- Point C (P=300, S=150): Profit = 10(300) + 18(150) = $3,000 + $2,700 = **$5,700**
- Point D (P=450, S=0): Profit = 10(450) + 18(0) = $4,500

**Finding Point C (intersection of both constraints):**

Set the two constraint equations as equalities and solve simultaneously:
```
2P + 4S = 1,200  ...(1)
2P + 2S = 900    ...(2)

Subtract (2) from (1):
2S = 300
S = 150

Substitute S = 150 into (2):
2P + 2(150) = 900
2P = 600
P = 300
```

**Optimal solution: Produce 300 potpourri bags and 150 scented candles for maximum profit of $5,700.**

## 3. CONTEMPORARY PLANNING TECHNIQUES

Two techniques appropriate for dynamic and complex environments:
1. Project Management
2. Scenario Planning

Both emphasize **flexibility**, which is important for making planning more effective and efficient.

---

### 3.1 Project Management

**Project:** A one-time-only set of activities that has a definite beginning and ending point in time. Projects vary in size and scope.

**Project management:** The task of getting a project's activities done on time, within budget, and according to specifications.

Organizations use project management because it fits well with the need for flexibility and rapid response to perceived market opportunities.

**When to use project management (vs. standard planning procedures):**
When projects are:
- Unique
- Have specific deadlines
- Contain complex interrelated tasks requiring specialized skills
- Temporary in nature

#### Project Management Process

**Typical structure:** Work is done by a project team whose members are assigned from their respective work areas and report to a project manager. When the project team accomplishes its goals, it disbands and members return to their permanent work areas or move to other projects.

**Essential steps in the project planning process (Exhibit PC-12):**

1. **Define objectives** - manager and team members need to know what is expected
2. **Identify activities and resources** - what materials and labor are needed? (may be time-consuming and complex for unique projects)
3. **Establish sequences** - determine the order of completion; what must be done before other things can start? What can be done simultaneously? (uses flowchart diagrams: Gantt chart, load chart, PERT network)
4. **Estimate time for activities** - time estimates for each activity
5. **Determine project completion date** - develop overall project schedule
6. **Compare with objectives** - compare schedule to goals and make adjustments if needed
7. **Determine additional resource requirements** - if completion time is too long, assign more resources to critical activities

Today, the project management process can take place online using web-based software packages (covering project accounting, estimating, scheduling, bug and defect tracking).

#### The Role of the Project Manager

- Managing projects is different from overseeing a production line or preparing routine tallies
- The one-shot nature of the work makes project managers the organizational equivalent of a "hired gunman" - there's a job to be done, it must be defined in detail, and the project manager is responsible for how it's done
- Even with sophisticated scheduling programs, the role remains difficult because the manager is managing people who are typically still assigned to their permanent work areas
- The project manager's only real influence is through **communication skills** and **power of persuasion**
- Team members usually work on two to three projects simultaneously, so project managers compete with each other for workers' attention

---

### 3.2 Scenario Planning

**Scenario:** A consistent view of what the future is likely to be.

- Also called **contingency planning** - "if this event happens, then we need to take these actions"
- Useful for anticipating events that CAN be anticipated

**Limitation of scenario planning:**
- Difficult to forecast **random events** - major surprises and aberrations that can't be foreseen
- Scenario planning is of little use for totally unexpected events that no one could have envisioned

**Dealing with unanticipatable events:**

1. Have an **early warning system** in place - early warning indicators give managers advance notice of potential risks and changes so they can take action
2. Have **appropriate responses (plans)** in place if unexpected events occur
