## 1. Stages of Creativity (Csikszentmihalyi, 1996)

**Csikszentmihalyi (1996)** observed that the creative process normally takes **five steps**:

| Stage | Name | Definition / Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Preparation** | Becoming immersed in problematic issues that are interesting and arouse curiosity |
| 2 | **Incubation** | Ideas churn around below the threshold of consciousness |
| 3 | **Insight** | The "Aha!" moment when the puzzle starts to fall together |
| 4 | **Evaluation** | Deciding if the insight is valuable and worth pursuing |
| 5 | **Elaboration** | Translating the insight into its final work |

## 2. Systems Approach to Creativity

### Core Argument

Creativity is not purely a mental process - it is equally a **cultural and social event**:

> "what we call creativity is not the product of a single individual, but of social systems making judgments about individual's products."

Creativity is a process that can be observed only at the intersection where **individuals**, **domains**, and **fields** interact.

### The Three Components

#### The Person (Individual Practitioner)
- Contributes **genetic makeup, talents, and experience**
- **Produces innovations** - novel variations in the content of the domain
- Some individuals are more likely to make changes either because of personal qualities or because they are well positioned with respect to the domain - better access to it, or social circumstances that allow free time to experiment

#### The Domain (Cultural System)
- The **cultural/symbolic** system consisting of **knowledge, tools, values, and practices**
- **Transmits the existing body of knowledge** to the Person
- For creativity to occur, "a set of rules and practices must be transmitted from the domain to the individual"
- The individual must then produce a novel variation in the content of the domain
- "Creativity occurs when a person makes a change in a domain, a change that will be transmitted through time"

#### The Field (Social System / Gatekeepers)
- The **social organization** of the domain - consists of **teachers, critics, journal editors, museum curators, agency directors, and foundation officers** who decide what belongs to a domain and what does not
- **Evaluates innovations and retains selected ones**
- "Changes are not adopted unless they are sanctioned by some group entitled to make decisions as to what should or should not be included in the domain"
- Example: "In physics, the opinion of a very small number of leading university professors was enough to certify that Einstein's ideas were creative."

### Social Valuation: Originality is NOT Creativity

> "As long as the idea or product has not been validated, we might have **originality**, but not **creativity**."

The two are inseparable:

> "there is no way, even in principle, to separate the reaction of society from the person's contribution. The two are inseparable."

**Bhawuk (2003)** argued that people strive to excel in areas which are valued in a culture. Example: spirituality is valued in India, which continues to produce eminent spiritual gurus.

## 3. Confluence Approach

### Definition

The **Confluence Approach** (Amabile, 1983) holds that:

> "for creativity to occur, **multiple components must converge**."

"Only when all these conditions are present can a high level of creativity emerge." (Sternberg & Lubart, 1996)

### The Six Distinct Resources (Lubart, 1994)

| # | Resource | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intellectual Abilities** | The ability to see problems in new ways; recognize which ideas are worth pursuing; and **persuasive skills** to convince others of new ideas |
| 2 | **Knowledge** | Enough knowledge about a field to move it forward |
| 3 | **Certain Styles of Thinking** | A preference for thinking in novel ways and an ability to see the big picture - to think **globally as well as locally** |
| 4 | **Personality Attributes** | Willingness to **take risks** and to **tolerate ambiguity** |
| 5 | **Intrinsic, Task-Focused Motivation** | Creative people love what they are doing and find **intrinsic rewards** in their work |
| 6 | **A Supportive Environment** | An environment that is supportive of creative ideas |

### Research Support (Lubart & Sternberg, 1995)

Study of 48 adults across four domains (writing, art, advertising, science). Three key findings:

1. Raters showed considerable agreement on what was and was not creative - confirming creativity can be studied scientifically
2. Creativity in one domain was only **moderately** related to creativity in other domains - people can be creative in one area but not another
3. Intellectual ability, thinking style, and personality were all **significantly related** to creativity

## 4. Fostering Creativity

### Divergent Thinking

> "To promote creativity, we need different view points. **Divergent thinking** is needed to produce as many ideas as possible."

### 12 Techniques (Nickerson, 1999)

| # | Technique |
|---|---|
| 1 | Establishing purpose and intention |
| 2 | Building basic skills |
| 3 | Encouraging acquisitions of domain-specific knowledge |
| 4 | Stimulating and rewarding curiosity and exploration |
| 5 | Building motivation, especially **internal motivation** |
| 6 | Encouraging confidence and a willingness to take risks |
| 7 | Focusing on mastery and self-competition |
| 8 | Promoting supportable beliefs about creativity |
| 9 | Providing opportunities for choice and discovery |
| 10 | Developing self-management (**metacognitive skills**) |
| 11 | Teaching techniques and strategies for facilitating creative performance |
| 12 | Providing balance |

### Additional Techniques (Torrance, 1995)

- Soft thinking
- Deconstruction / reconstruction
- Imaging
- Playing
- Exploration
- Dialectical thinking
- Learning from failure
- Collaboration / cooperation

### Four Key Abilities in Creative Problem-Solving

| Ability | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Fluency** | The elaboration of many ideas, alternatives, or solutions |
| **Flexibility** | The ability to process information or objects in **different ways** given the same stimulus |
| **Originality** | Getting away from the obvious and commonplace from habit-bound thinking |
| **Elaboration** | The ability to **embellish ideas with details** |

### Indian Context

**Misra, Srivastava, and Misra (2006)** speak of Indian intuitive creativity with emphasis on cultural variation in conceptualization, manifestation, and societal organization.

- Creativity requires **sensitivity** and **independence**; in the Indian tradition, sensitivity is a **feminine virtue** while independence is a **masculine one** - creating a cultural source of conflict for developing creativity
- **Gender differences in creativity are mixed:**
  - No gender difference: Das (1996), Kapoor (1996), Verma (1993)
  - Boys more creative than girls: Agarwal & Agarwal (1999), Hussain & Sinha (1995)
